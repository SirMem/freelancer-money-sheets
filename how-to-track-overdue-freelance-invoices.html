What “aging” means for a solo freelancer

Answer: Aging groups open invoice balances by days past the due date so you chase late cash in order, not by vibe.

Aging groups open balances by days past the due date. Use these four buckets (they match the free sample):

BucketMeaningFriday action
CurrentNot past dueNo chase; optional pre-due note on large AP
1–30Recently latePolite reminder + payment details
31–60Seriously lateAsk what is blocking payment; firmer tone
60+At riskFinal written chase; pause new work if that is your policy

Start with the oldest meaningful unpaid balances, not only the largest client. A $400 invoice at 45 days often beats an $8k invoice at 5 days when cash is tight.

Friday 10-minute ritual

Answer: Once a week: confirm payments, filter unpaid, sort by days overdue, send one chase per row due today, log outcome, glance at bucket totals.

  1. Open the sheet. Confirm every payment that landed this week (same day you see the bank hit).
  2. Filter Status ≠ Paid. Sort by days overdue descending, then amount.
  3. Send one chase per overdue row that is due today on Next chase — not a batch of guilt emails.
  4. Log channel + outcome; set the next chase date (due / +7 / +14 / +21–30).
  5. Glance at bucket totals: where is cash stuck this week?

FAQ

Do I need QuickBooks to track overdue freelance invoices?
No. A spreadsheet with aging buckets and a Friday chase list is enough for a solo freelancer with a handful of open invoices. Move to accounting software when you need tax-ready books or multi-user AR — not to start seeing who is late.
How often should freelancers chase overdue invoices?
Run one 10-minute ritual every Friday: confirm bank hits, sort unpaid by days overdue, send one chase per row whose Next chase is today, then set the next date (+7 / +14 / +21–30). Daily guilt batches burn relationship capital.
What aging buckets should a solo freelancer use?
Four buckets: Current (not past due), 1–30, 31–60, and 60+ days past the due date. Chase oldest meaningful balances first when cash is tight — a $400 at 45 days often beats an $8k at 5 days.
