What “categorize” means for a solo freelancer

Answer: Assign every business spend to one editable Schedule C–style label and a business-use %, then sum spent and deductible by category for the month.

You are not recreating a CPA firm. You need three facts per row:

FactWhy
CategoryMatches how you (or your preparer) will group the year — advertising, supplies, phone, meals…
AmountFull receipt, not “about $40”
Business-use %Phone / internet / meals are rarely 100%; deductible = amount × %

Minimum columns (do not skip)

Answer: Date, vendor, description, category, amount, business-use %, deductible (formula), plus year/month helpers for rollups.

ColumnWhy
DateMonth filters and audit trail
VendorMatch bank lines later

AmountFull spend before %
Business-use %0–100; drives deductible
DeductibleAmount × % (formula — do not hard-type)

Monthly 15-minute ritual

Answer: Once a month: dump new receipts, assign category + %, glance at category totals, fix miscategorized rows, export or screenshot the month for your archive.

  1. Open the Categories sheet. Confirm labels still match how you work (rename freely for your jurisdiction).
  2. Paste or type new Expense Log rows for the month (yellow input cells).
  3. Set business-use % honestly (phone, meals, mixed-use software).
  4. Check month totals by category: spent vs deductible.
  5. Fix “Misc” dumps — misc is a parking lot, not a strategy.

FAQ

Do freelancers need QuickBooks to categorize expenses?
No. A one-month spreadsheet with Schedule C–style categories and a business-use % column is enough to see spent vs deductible before you buy accounting software. Move to QuickBooks or similar when you need multi-user books, bank sync, or accountant handoff — not to start labeling receipts.
What expense categories should a solo freelancer use?
Start with common Schedule C (Form 1040) line labels: advertising, office expense, supplies, utilities, travel, meals, phone/internet, software, professional services, and education. Keep the list editable; rename for your jurisdiction. The free sample ships with these labels preloaded.
How do I handle partial business use like phone or meals?
Log the full amount, then set business-use % so deductible = amount × %. On the free sample EXAMPLE: phone at 60% and meals at 50% turn $422.69 spent into $369.69 deductible. Adjust % to your facts; this is not tax advice.
